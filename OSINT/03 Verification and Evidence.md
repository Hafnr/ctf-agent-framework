# Verification and Evidence

## Verification standard

For an important claim, seek at least two independent lines of evidence. Two websites repeating the same original report are one line, not two.

## Source evaluation

Ask:

- Who created it and how would they know?
- Is it primary, contemporaneous, and complete?
- Has it been edited or reposted?
- What incentives or biases exist?
- Can date, location, and identity be verified independently?
- What evidence would disprove the claim?

## Preserve

- original URL and access time;
- archived URL where possible;
- screenshot including context, not only a cropped claim;
- downloaded file and hash when permitted;
- exact search query and tool;
- translation method and original text;
- reasoning chain and alternative explanations.

## Confidence language

Use calibrated terms such as confirmed, highly likely, likely, possible, or unsupported, and explain why. Avoid turning an inference into a fact through repeated paraphrasing.
